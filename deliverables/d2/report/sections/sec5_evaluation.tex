\hdr{Evaluation objective and D2 scope.} The primary objective of the evaluation in this D2 report is to demonstrate that the end-to-end agentic pipeline and automated evaluation harness built by the team run reliably and produce reproducible, auditable baseline measurements across configurations. Further experiments planned for \textbf{D3} (Section~\ref{sec:plan}) will evaluate harder unseen multi-hop queries where lexical retrieval is not saturated, reduce verifier over-refusals under the 2-repair budget, benchmark dense retrieval, and test adversarial prompt injections.

\hdr{Benchmarks.} Each self-built benchmark has 50 Vietnamese questions over its corpus (100 now; D3 adds 50--100 unseen harder questions and $\ge$30 attack cases, Section~\ref{sec:plan}): 40 single-record, 5 two-record and 5 out-of-scope questions to refuse. Each item includes gold record IDs, a reference answer, and verbatim quotes, locked by SHA-256 hash prior to data collection.

Candidate questions were drafted with an LLM, cross-checked by two independent LLM verifiers and an adjudicator, and validated programmatically for exact quote matching against the corpus, before Võ Phạm Tuấn Dũng manually reviewed all 100 items against the source PDFs on 23 October. Because the questions were synthesized from regulatory provisions rather than collected from live users, our post-hoc quality audit noted three limitations despite zero gold-label errors:
\begin{itemize}
\item plain BM25 on the raw question already ranks a gold record first for 89\% (HCMUT regulations) and 78\% (labour law) of questions, so retrieval cannot separate the configurations;
\item about one third of the answerable questions can be answered without the corpus (LLM estimate);
\item 22 HCMUT questions pin a document by a decision number taken from a web listing.
\end{itemize}

\hdr{Evaluation metrics.} \emph{Strict success} counts a trial only if the run completed, the action was appropriate (answer when the corpus answers, refuse when it does not), the answer is judged correct and complete, and every factual claim is supported by the cited sources. Execution errors and failed judgments count as failures, so the reported rate is a conservative lower bound over all planned trials. Strict success is scored by the offline LLM judge and calibrated against our blind human audit below.

Three behavioural metrics require no LLM judge: behaviour match, over-refusal rate, and unsafe answer rate. Retrieval Recall@5 (whether gold articles appear in retrieved results) and citation F1 (overlap between cited and gold article IDs) evaluate document-level grounding independently of answer correctness. All confidence intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals over the 50 questions after averaging across the three seeds; at $N=50$, differences under roughly 10 percentage points fall within sampling noise.

\hdr{LLM judge and human calibration audit.} An LLM judge (DeepSeek V4 Flash, accessed via an API gateway) scores each output blind to the system configuration and verifier outcome (offline only; model revision not pinned~\citep{deepseek2026api}; LLM judges can exhibit systematic biases~\citep{zheng2023judge}). On a blind 60-item human audit (30 per benchmark, 4B outputs), strict-success agreement is 96.7\% (29/30, $\kappa = 0.93$; weighted 98.8\%, $\kappa_w = 0.97$) on HCMUT and 90.0\% (27/30, $\kappa = 0.80$; weighted 97.5\%, $\kappa_w = 0.95$) on labour law; behaviour agreement is 100.0\% and 93.3\% ($\kappa=0.84$). All four disagreements are documented in the repository. Separating correctness from citation support follows~\citet{gao2023alce}.

\hdr{Experimental setup and baselines.} The full system (verifier and reranker) is compared against two ablations: \emph{no verifier} (neither verifier nor reranker) and \emph{verifier} (verifier with repair, no reranker). Each configuration runs across three random seeds on all 50 questions per benchmark.

Two single-seed diagnostic baselines bracket the system configurations: \emph{closed-book} (question only, no tools or corpus access) and \emph{oracle} (gold records provided directly in context, evaluated on the 45 answerable questions). Each controller size is therefore evaluated across 900 system trials and 190 baseline trials (1{,}090 logged trials per controller). Both the 4B controller (default) and the 2B controller (lightweight option) are evaluated under the same protocol. Accuracy runs were collected on a shared server GPU (NVIDIA H100), while interactive latency was measured sequentially on an Apple M4 laptop (16\,GiB).

\hdr{(1) Controller scale drives answer synthesis and citation quality (Figure~\ref{fig:bars}).} For the full system (\emph{verifier + reranker}), strict success is 60.7\% [50--71] on HCMUT regulations and 56.7\% [47--67] on labour law with the 4B controller, compared with 28.7\% [20--37] and 28.0\% [19--37] with the 2B controller.

Scaling the controller from 2B to 4B on the same pipeline adds $+32.0$ percentage points [21.3, 42.0] on HCMUT and $+28.7$ [18.0, 39.3] on labour law for the full system ($+38.7$ and $+30.7$ without verifier; $+40.0$ and $+24.7$ with verifier only). All six paired bootstrap differences across the three configurations and two benchmarks exclude zero.

\hdr{(2) Effect of the rule-based citation verifier on citation integrity and audited accuracy.} In Table~\ref{tab:main}, under the LLM judge alone (\emph{judge-only strict success}, which grades answer prose without verifying exact substrings against the corpus), \emph{No verifier} reaches 70.0\% and 62.0\% compared with 60.7\% and 56.7\% for the full system, because the verifier converts unresolved citation errors after two repair attempts into refusals.

Table~\ref{tab:diag} explains this trade-off: without the verifier, \textbf{25.6\% (31/121) of HCMUT answers and 28.0\% (37/132) of labour-law answers from the 4B controller} (and 39.8\% and 45.9\% from the 2B controller) \textbf{contain unverifiable citations}, such as non-verbatim quotes, unretrieved section IDs, or numbers drawn from adjacent clauses. Because the LLM judge does not perform exact substring or local-window numeric checks against the full PDF corpus, it counts 22 (HCMUT) and 16 (labour law) of those answers as strictly successful. When strict success additionally requires passing deterministic L1 citation verification (\emph{Audited strict} in Table~\ref{tab:diag}), enabling the verifier \textbf{reduces unverified citations to 0.0\%} (0/109 on HCMUT and 0/111--108 on labour law), corrects 17--20 initially rejected drafts through bounded repair, and \textbf{increases citation-audited strict success from 55.3\% to 64.0\% ($+8.7$\,pp, Verifier) and 60.7\% ($+5.4$\,pp, Full) on HCMUT, and from 51.3\% to 56.7\% ($+5.4$\,pp, Full) on labour law}. Because Recall@5 is already 0.98--0.99 without the reranker on these 100 questions, we retain both components and will evaluate the reranker on a harder unseen benchmark in Week 45.

\hdr{(3) Baselines.} Without corpus access, the 4B model scores 2.0\% [0--6] on HCMUT regulations and 24.0\% [12--36] on labour law, indicating that pre-training knowledge contributes partially on general labour law. When provided with the gold records directly, the oracle baseline reaches 82.2\% [71--93] and 75.6\% [62--87] on the 45 answerable questions (compared with 69.6\% and 60.7\% for \emph{No verifier}), showing that roughly 18--24\% of answerable questions still fail at multi-clause synthesis even under ideal retrieval. Across all 1,090 trials, 17 ended as execution errors (wall-time limit) and are counted as failures.

\hdr{Error analysis and experimental roadmap for D3.} Three failure modes account for the remaining errors in the 4B full system: (i)~\emph{repair-budget refusals} (13/150 HCMUT and 18/150 labour trials refused after two L1 quote/number rejections on answerable questions), (ii)~\emph{multi-clause synthesis omissions} (oracle gap of 18--24\% when gold articles are provided), and (iii)~\emph{wall-time budget timeouts} (17 of 1,090 trials). Because D2 evaluation focuses on establishing a working, measurable baseline and lexical retrieval is near ceiling on this initial 100-question set, D3 will target these bottlenecks on harder unseen multi-hop and paraphrased benchmarks, refine the repair loop, and execute entry-point attack tests.

\hdr{Cost, memory footprint, and latency.} The controller and reranker run locally with zero per-call API cost. On the 16\,GiB Apple M4 laptop, total resident memory (RSS) for the active baseline stack is approximately 4.8\,GB with the 4B controller ($\approx 3.4$\,GB for the 4B \texttt{llama-server}, 1.2\,GB for the cross-encoder reranker, and 0.2\,GB for the FastAPI web server and in-memory lexical indices) and 2.8\,GB with the 2B controller ($\approx 1.4$\,GB for the 2B server); enabling the dense vector search module with \texttt{EmbeddingGemma-2} ($\approx 300$M parameters, \texttt{bfloat16}) adds $\approx 0.5$\,GB ($517$\,MB of model weights, bringing the total to $\approx 5.3$\,GB on 4B and $3.3$\,GB on 2B). For the full system, the controller reads on average 12,070 (HCMUT regulations) and 11,477 (labour law) input tokens and generates 763 and 865 output tokens per trial across 3.4 and 3.7 model calls. Offline LLM judging cost USD 1.06 and USD 1.10 across the two 545-trial runs as reported by the gateway. Collecting each run took 34 and 33 minutes of wall time on the shared H100 (GPU-active hours were not measured separately). In sequential single-query runs on the Apple M4 laptop over 50 questions per benchmark, the full system answers in a median of 33.0\,s (HCMUT regulations, p95 86\,s) and 29.8\,s (labour law, p95 72\,s) with the 4B controller ($\approx 43$ tokens/s), and in 13.3\,s (p95 30\,s) and 14.6\,s (p95 28\,s) with the 2B controller ($\approx 92$ tokens/s).
