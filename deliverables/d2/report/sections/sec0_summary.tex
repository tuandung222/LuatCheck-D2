\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{\small
\textbf{Summary.} LuatCheck answers a question only from a document collection, cites the sections it used, checks each citation against the source text in code, and refuses or asks for clarification when the sources do not answer. The current system is one bounded agent. Its controller is a 4-billion-parameter model that runs on a laptop (a lighter 2B option exists). It uses read-only search and read tools, keyword retrieval with a neural reranker, a citation verifier with bounded repair, and input guardrails.\par\smallskip
\textbf{Evaluated:} two benchmarks of 50 Vietnamese questions each (HCMUT regulations, labour law), three configurations (no verifier, verifier, verifier and reranker), three random seeds, plus two diagnostic baselines; 1{,}090 recorded trials per system. Strict success means appropriate behaviour, a correct and complete answer, and every claim supported by the sources. An LLM judges it (lower bound over all trials); on a blind 60-item human audit (30 per benchmark), judge--human agreement on strict success is 96.7\% ($\kappa=0.93$) on HCMUT regulations and 90.0\% ($\kappa=0.80$) on labour law.\par\smallskip
\textbf{Result:} the current system reaches 60.7\% (HCMUT regulations) and 56.7\% (labour law) strict success; the initial system, which used the 2B controller, reached 14.0\% and 18.7\%. The revision was designed after we analysed the initial system's failures on these same questions, so its gains may be optimistic; we will confirm them on a new set of questions for the final report. In the ablation, neither the verifier nor the reranker raised strict success on these benchmarks.}}
