\section{Introduction, Motivation, and Delta from Proposal}

\hdr{Vietnamese Legal Stakes \& Enterprise Context.} Legal consultations in Vietnam carry substantial regulatory, financial, and civil liability. Under the Vietnamese Labor Code 2019 (Law No.~45/2019/QH14), improper termination notices, unlawful overtime mandates, or unauthorized wage deductions subject enterprises to punitive administrative fines and mandatory back-pay. Non-lawyer stakeholders---such as human resources (HR) administrators, small-business managers, and employees---frequently require rapid, intelligible legal interpretations coupled with verified statutory grounding. However, deploying public, commercial large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT or Claude poses two severe risks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=14pt,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Data Privacy \& Regulatory Non-Compliance:} Vietnamese enterprises face strict statutory restrictions under the \textit{Personal Data Protection Law 2025} (PDPL 2025, Law No.~91/2025/QH15)~\citep{vietnam2025pdpl} and Decree No.~356/2025/ND-CP~\citep{decree3562025}. Inquiring into employment disputes requires pasting sensitive contracts containing Citizen IDs, salary figures, and medical histories. Sending such personally identifiable information (PII) to foreign cloud-hosted LLM endpoints violates corporate data residency and statutory obligations.
    \item \textbf{Hallucinated Citations \& Legal Liability:} Recent empirical benchmarks demonstrate that state-of-the-art commercial legal AI tools fabricate or mis-attribute statutory provisions in over 15--30\% of queries~\citep{magesh2025hallucination}. In law, a plausible but fictitious article number is far more hazardous than an explicit refusal.
\end{enumerate}

\hdr{LuatCheck Positioning.} LuatCheck resolves this dilemma by executing entirely on local, organization-controlled hardware (Apple Silicon / Metal GPU) using quantized open-weight models (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct). It replaces unchecked generation with an agentic, evidence-grounded control loop featuring deterministic citation verification, least-privilege tool isolation, and a principled refusal path.

\hdr{Delta from Proposal \& Milestone Status.} The project is currently \textbf{ON TRACK}. Between the D1 proposal in Week 2 and this D2 checkpoint (Week 7), the end-to-end ReAct finite-state machine, Model Context Protocol (MCP) server, deterministic L1 verifier, and multi-seed evaluation harnesses were fully implemented. Following the course's \textit{"learning through working"} principle, we made two technical scope adjustments:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=14pt,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Prioritization of L1 Deterministic Verification over L2 NLI:} Our initial tests revealed that cross-encoder NLI models on Vietnamese legal text add $1.8$\,s latency per claim while providing marginal benefit over token-level span support. We therefore established the deterministic L1 verifier (existence + provenance + span overlap) as the non-negotiable floor, deferring heavy L2 NLI to the Week 10 D3 ablation.
    \item \textbf{Standardization on FastMCP:} All tool definitions were migrated to the Model Context Protocol (\texttt{FastMCP}) standard, formalizing least-privilege permission gating and human-in-the-loop (HITL) barriers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and System Positioning}

\hdr{Vietnamese Legal NLP Benchmarks.} Early Vietnamese legal processing concentrated on statutory information retrieval and named-entity recognition within the VLSP shared tasks~\citep{vlsp2021legal}. Recently, Nguyen et al. released VLQA~\citep{vlqa2025}, the first comprehensive benchmark for Vietnamese legal question answering. While VLQA establishes gold-standard query-article pairs, existing evaluations focus primarily on single-turn dense retrieval or direct generation, lacking agentic multi-hop retrieval and claim-level verification.

\hdr{Attributed Generation \& Agentic RAG.} Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds language models in external knowledge~\citep{lewis2020rag}. The ALCE benchmark~\citep{gao2023alce} formalized citation precision and recall for LLMs. However, standard RAG retrieves once without self-correction. Yao et al.~\citep{yao2023react} introduced ReAct, interleaving reasoning traces with tool actions. Crucially, Huang et al.~\citep{huang2024selfcorrect} proved that small language models cannot reliably self-correct reasoning without external, deterministic grounding. Furthermore, tool-integrated agents face indirect prompt injection vulnerabilities~\citep{greshake2023indirect,debenedetti2024agentdojo}. As Kapoor et al.~\citep{kapoor2024agentsmatter} emphasize, agentic claims must be substantiated with strict variance, latency, and cost reporting.

\hdr{Budgeted ReAct Finite-State Machine (R1).} The central control loop operates as a deterministic finite-state machine~\citep{yao2023react}. To prevent infinite loops and runaway latency in small models, the agent enforces a hard budget cap of $\le 5$ tool executions and $\le 2$ targeted repair rounds. The action space is strictly schema-constrained via Pydantic:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=14pt,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]
    \item \texttt{search\_law(query, top\_k=5)}: Hybrid retrieval combining BM25 and dense embeddings via Reciprocal Rank Fusion ($k=60$).
    \item \texttt{get\_article(article\_id)}: Retrieves verbatim statutory text for a canonical identifier (e.g., \texttt{dieu\_35}).
    \item \texttt{answer(content, citations)}: Formulates candidate legal advice referencing specific cited provisions.
    \item \texttt{clarify(question)}: Inquires for missing case details when statutory conditions cannot be evaluated.
    \item \texttt{refuse(reason)}: Explicit, polite refusal triggered when queries solicit illegal conduct or lack legal foundation.
\end{itemize}

\hdr{MCP Toolset and Least Privilege Matrix (R2).} The tool subsystem is exposed as a Model Context Protocol server via \texttt{FastMCP}, enforcing a three-tier permission matrix (Table~\ref{tab:tools}). Read-only tools execute autonomously, while the irreversible egress action (\texttt{send\_summary}) is unconditionally paused by an execution gate requiring human UI approval and an idempotency token.

\hdr{Dual-Tier Memory Architecture (R3).} LuatCheck decouples short-term session state from durable organizational storage:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=14pt,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Short-Term Case Ledger:} An in-memory Pydantic model maintaining extracted client facts, issued queries, active session article cache, and remaining tool/repair budgets.
    \item \textbf{Long-Term Episodic Store (SQLite):} Persists verified consultations. \textit{Write Policy:} Only terminal answers that pass 100\% of L1 verification are committed; all PII (phone numbers, emails, Citizen IDs) is automatically redacted via regex per PDPL 2025. \textit{Retrieval Policy:} Lookup requires cosine similarity $\ge 0.85$ and an exact statutory corpus version match (\texttt{v2026.1}). Cached records undergo compulsory L1 re-verification prior to display.
\end{itemize}

\hdr{L1 Deterministic Evidence Verifier (R4).} When the model proposes \texttt{answer}, execution passes to the L1 verifier:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=14pt,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Statutory Existence Check:} Ensures all cited IDs exist within the validated legal corpus (Law No.~45/2019/QH14).
    \item \textbf{Retrieval Provenance Check:} Validates that cited provisions were genuinely fetched during the active session.
    \item \textbf{Sentence Span Overlap Support Check:} Tokenizes generated sentences and computes n-gram overlap against cited text (threshold $\ge 0.25$). Statements failing overlap trigger targeted repair prompts back to the ReAct agent.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=14pt,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]

SQL injection payloads (\texttt{INDIRECT\_03}) were rendered inert by SQLite parameterization and PII sanitization.
\end{itemize}
